# Kapil Billing — Architectural Decisions Record

> **Purpose:** Durable record of foundational architectural decisions, their rationale, and trade-offs.

1. **Three-Module Isolation Rule:** Sales, Purchase, and Casting items and party tables are strictly isolated with no foreign keys or name-matching between them because of the owner's rule: three separate worlds, nothing linked even if names match.
2. **Integer Monetary Units in Purchase:** Purchase calculations use integer grams (`weight_g`), milli-rupees (`rate_milli`), and paise (`amount_paise`) because floating-point rounding errors on multi-ton metal deliveries accumulate into substantial monetary discrepancies.
3. **Floating-point Indian Rupees in Sales:** Sales billing uses standard floating-point rupees because tax invoices require standard Indian GST percentage calculations, half-up rounding, and standard roundoff reconciliation.
4. **Dual Kacha & GST Supplier Pools:** Suppliers maintain two separate, non-netted balance pools (`opening_kacha_paise` and `opening_gst_paise`) because official tax-invoice purchases and internal scrap transactions operate under separate settlement rhythms.
5. **FIFO Plus Manual Pin Settlement:** Supplier bill settlements default to FIFO allocation while allowing operators to manually pin payments to specific bills so specific invoices can be cleared or disputed out of order without corrupting running balances.
6. **Bardan (Bags) Settlement per Entry:** Bardan is settled per entry: `deduct` (credit = bag kg × chosen line rate), `in_kind` (no money, settled with note), `cash_back` (memo amount only, no effect on payable), and `advance` (a `bardan_advance` credit in the bill's pool); several entries can exist per bill, and a bill remains "bardan pending" until at least one entry exists.
7. **Soft-Delete for Parties (Customers & Suppliers):** Customers and suppliers are never hard-deleted (`active: false`) because deleting a party record would break historical ledger integrity, transaction logs, and financial statement generation.
8. **Delete-Blocking for Used Items:** Items referenced in any bill, line, or session cannot be hard-deleted (only deactivated) to prevent orphaned foreign keys and corrupt bill reproductions, while unreferenced scratch items delete cleanly.
9. **Tabbed Consolidation on Items and Party Ledger:** Items (`/items` with 3 tabs) and Party Ledger (`/ledger` with 2 tabs) replace fragmented individual pages so the operator has unified operational hubs with zero cross-module linking.
10. **Dedicated Log Windows (`LogsShell`):** Transaction log views use a default 90-day time window and paginated shell (`LogsShell`) so historical databases with tens of thousands of rows render instantly without bogging down browser memory.
11. **Database-Backed Module Settings:** Module toggles are stored in PocketBase (`app_settings` collection) rather than browser `localStorage` so disabling or enabling a module synchronizes instantly across all operator devices (PC, tablet, mobile).
12. **Windows Local-Primary Architecture:** The primary production database runs locally on the factory Windows PC under SQLite WAL mode rather than in the cloud to guarantee zero-latency billing and 100% offline operational resilience during factory network outages.
13. **Muse Writes Drafts Only:** AI workers parse WhatsApp bill photos and bank SMS into `bill_drafts` and `payment_drafts` without direct write permissions to accounting tables, ensuring the business owner remains the sole approving financial authority.
14. **Strict Pool Separation in Matching:** Kacha and GST pools never cross during automatic FIFO allocation, and manual payment allocation pins must strictly stay inside the payment's own pool to preserve tax accounting integrity.
15. **Computed Whole-Rupee Bill Round-off:** A bill net amount is always rounded to a whole rupee using automated round-off calculation (never manual) so ledger entries avoid fraction-of-a-rupee cash discrepancies.
16. **GST vs RCM Liability Treatment:** Standard GST is added directly to the supplier payable amount, while Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) is displayed as our own tax liability to the government and is not added to the supplier payable.
17. **Granular Payment Kinds:** Outward payments support distinct kinds (`payment`, `paid_on_behalf`, `settle_off`, `refund_received`, `bardan_advance`) to represent true business operations like paying transporters/labour on a supplier's behalf, penny write-offs, or refunds without shoehorning them into generic payment rows.
18. **FY-Based Immutable Inward Numbers:** Inward purchase numbers (`in_no`) follow an annual financial year sequence (e.g. `IN-2627-0001`) and are never reused; cancelled bills retain their sequence number for complete auditability.
19. **Internal-Only Market Benchmarks & Private Notes:** Market rate premiums against Jamnagar vilaity and private notes are strictly internal management metadata and are never printed on physical chitties or included in external PDF/JPG exports.
20. **No Automated Inventory Linking (No Auto-Stock):** Raw scrap purchases, casting wire yields, and finished brass goods are never automatically coupled in software because physical metal grades, burning losses, and casting yields require manual verification.
21. **Title Case Master Item Names with Tab-Local Uniqueness:** Item names are normalized to Title Case and validated for uniqueness strictly within their own master tab, allowing identical naming (e.g. "Brass Scrap") to coexist cleanly across separate modules.
22. **Full-Page Chitty Inspection:** Viewing a purchase bill uses a dedicated full-screen page (`/purchase-bills/$billId`) rather than a side drawer to ensure complete line inspection, print alignment, and uncluttered keyboard navigation.
23. **Versioned Casting Ready Cost:** New Casting sessions use metal cost/kg + twice 7% of the Casting chitty Brass rate/kg + twice coal cost/wire kg + twice worker cost/wire kg. Repeated Brass lines use their weight-based average rate on that same chitty. A session without Brass has zero Baravo. Existing sessions retain version 1 (7.5% of average metal cost) when viewed, edited, or restored; no historical data is rewritten.
